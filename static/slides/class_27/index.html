AR, MA, and ARMA

Definitions

  • \(\mathrm{AR}(p)\)

    \[ (y_i - \mu) = \sum_{j = 1}^p \phi_j (y_{i - j} - \mu) + \varepsilon_i \]

  • \(\mathrm{MA}(q)\)

    \[ y_i - \mu = \sum_{k = 1}^q \theta_k \varepsilon_{i - k} + \varepsilon_i \]

  • \(\mathrm{ARMA}(p, q)\)

    \[ (y_i - \mu) = \sum_{j = 1}^p \phi_j (y_{i - j} - \mu) + \sum_{k = 1}^q \theta_k \varepsilon_{i - k} + \varepsilon_i \]

Tests for Stationarity

  • We use “unit-root” tests for stationarity of \(\mathrm{AR}(p)\) processes:
    • The reference is to the characteristic equation for the autoregressive process with coefficients \(\phi_i\)
      • The characteristic equation is a polynomial of degree \(p\)
      • For a stationary process, all the roots of the characteristic equation will be less than one.
  • All \(\mathrm{MA}(q)\) processes are stationary, as long as \(\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma)\)
  • Unit-root tests in R:
    • For a data frame \(df\) with columns \(t\) for the time and \(y\) for the value.
    • Use the package feasts
      • Kwiatkowski–Phillips–Schmidt–Shin (KPSS) test: unitroot_kpss(df$y)
        • Null hypothesis \(H_0\): stationary
        • Alternate hypothesis \(H_a\): nonstationary
      • Phillips-Perron (PP) test: unitroot_pp(df$y)
        • Null hypothesis \(H_0\): nonstationary
        • Alternate hypothesis \(H_a\): stationary
    • This can be confusing because we have to interpret the tests in opposite ways:
      • KPSS: \(p < 0.05\) means nonstationary
      • PP: \(p < 0.05\) means stationary

Box-Jenkins Method

Box-Jenkins Method

  • Plot ACF and PACF
    • Stationarity:
      • Stationary: ACF decays to zero fairly quickly
      • Nonstationary: ACF decays very slowly
    • AR(p):
      • ACF is decaying exponential or oscillation
      • PACF is significant at lag \(p\), maybe earlier too, but nothing after \(p\)
    • MA(q):
      • PACF is decaying exponential or oscillation
      • ACF is significant at lag \(q\), maybe earlier too, but nothing after \(q\)
    • ARMA(p,q):
      • Both ACF and PACF are decaying exponential or oscillation

ARMA Processes

  • \(\mathrm{AR}(p)\) and \(\mathrm{MA}(q)\) processes let you estimate \(p\) or \(q\) from ACF and PACF using Box-Jenkins
  • It’s hard to estimate \(p\) and \(q\) for an ARMA process
    • Use regression and estimate AIC for different \(p\) and \(q\).
      • Choose the model with smallest AIC
    • Use cross-validation and estimate \(R^2\) and RMSE,
      • Choose the model with largest \(R^2\) or smallest RMSE.

  • R: fable and feasts packages:

    library(fable)
library(feasts)

feasts vs. modeltime

  • There are two important families of packages for statistical modeling of time series:
    • tidyverts by Rob Hyndeman, consists of tsibble, fable, and feasts. These packages provide one way to integrate time-series models into the tidyverse.
      • They are very powerful, but don’t integrate with the tidymodels framework.
      • To use the model-analysis in fable and feasts, you need to convert your data frames into tsibbles, which extend the tibble data frame into a time-series
  • modeltime integrates time-series models with tidymodels, but it isn’t as flexible about time-series:
    • All time series have to convert the time variable to date or datetime formats.
      • They don’t work with time-series that have numerical values for the time veriable.
      • They have powerful functions for cross-validation and model selection that are not available in the tidyverts framework.
      • The timetk package has a lot of useful functions for working with modeltime time-series.
