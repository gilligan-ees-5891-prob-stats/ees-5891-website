Statistical Models

  • What is a statistical model?
    • A model that represents:
      • Distributions of stochastic variables (e.g., measurements)
      • Relations among variables (e.g., covariance)
    • We generally expect a statistical model to be useful for making predictions, based on past observations

  • Mathematical formulation

    \[X \sim \mathcal{D}(\Theta)\]

    \(X\) represents observations, and the model says they are drawn from a statistical distribution \(\mathcal{D}(\Theta)\), with parameters \(\Theta\).

    • We can’t predict the specific values of a sample \(X\), but we expect that
      • As the # of observations becomes large,
      • The distribution of \(X\) will increasingly look like the parent distribution \(\mathcal{D}\).
      • This is the Law of Large Numbers

Global Warming from Satellite Measurements

  1. Satellites observe intensity of microwave radiation from earth
    • Model of bolometer instrument to convert voltage to microwave intensity
  2. Microwave intensity is an integral of emissions from all layers of atmosphere
    • Mathematical model of the atmosphere to calculate emissions from each layer
  3. Mathematical physics model: Stefan-Boltzmann Law: \(I = \sigma_{\mathrm{SB}} \epsilon T^4\), where
    • \(I\) is the emitted microwave intensity,
    • \(T\) is the temperature in Kelvin,
    • \(\epsilon\) is the microwave emissivity,
    • \(\sigma_{\mathrm{SB}}\) is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant
    • Calculate \(T\) from \(I\).
  1. Assemble snapshots of microwave intensity for each spot over the earth into a map of the whole earth.
    • Some data will be missing
  2. Statistical model of global average temperature from available data
  3. Statistical model of monthly data, over many years, to assess warming trend.
    • Account for random variation
    • Account for autocorrelation (warm years tend to follow warm years)

Linear Regression

Linear Regression

  • What is Regression?
    • Statistical modeling

      • Observations:

        • \(N\) observations:
          • Dependent variable \(Y = \{y_i\}\), \(i = 1, \ldots, N\)
          • Independent variables (predictors) \(X = \{x_{i,j}\}\), \(j = 1, \ldots, p\)

      • Statistical Model:

        \[ \begin{align} Y =& f(X) + \varepsilon \\ \varepsilon \sim& \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma) \end{align} \]

        • \(\varepsilon\) represents randomness (residuals, noise, errors, etc.)

  • Predictions:

    • New observations of \(X\)

      • Predict what \(Y\) should be for the new \(X\):

        \[ E(Y | X) = f(X) \]

  • Special case: Linear Regression

    \[ E(y_i|x_{i,j}) = f(x_{i,j}) = \beta_0 + \sum_{j = 1}^p \beta_j x_{i,j} \]

    • Linear Regression vs. Least-Squares:
      • Same math, different emphasis
      • Least-squares: estimate relationships in observed data
      • Regression: predict future data.

Assessing Model Quality

Regression Residuals

  • Residuals are the differences between predictions \(\hat{y}_i\) and observed \(y_i\):

    \[ \begin{align} y_i =& \hat{y}_i + \varepsilon_i \\ \hat{y}_i =& \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i \\ \varepsilon_i =& y_i - \hat{y}_i \end{align} \]

  • Residual testing:

    • Normality: Residuals should be normally distributed (use Q-Q plot)
    • Homogeneity: Residuals should not show structure (e.g., _heteroskedasticity)
    • Independence: Residuals should be non-persistent (we’ll cover this when we look at time series)

  • Checking normality for tree ring data:

    paleo_res <- paleo |> filter(! is.na(t_obs)) |> 
  mutate(res = t_obs - t_pred)

    shapiro.test(paleo_res$res)

Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

  • Basic Model:

    \[ \begin{align} y_i =& \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i + \varepsilon_i \\ \overline{y} =& \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i = 1}^N y_i~(\text{mean}) \\ \hat{y}_i =& \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i~(\text{prediction})\\ \varepsilon_i =& y_i - \hat{y}_i~(\text{error}) \end{align} \]

  • Mean Squared Error (MSE):

    \[ S_\varepsilon^2 = \frac{1}{N - 2} \sum_{i = 1}^N \varepsilon_i^2 \]

  • Sums of Squares:

    \[ \begin{align} \text{Total:}~\mathrm{SST} =& \sum_{i = 1}^N (y_i - \overline{y})^2 \\ \text{Regression:}~\mathrm{SSR} =& \sum_{i = 1}^N (\hat{y}_i - \overline{y})^2 \\ \text{Error:}~\mathrm{SSE} =& \sum_{i = 1}^N (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 = \sum_{i = 1}^N \varepsilon_i^2 \\ \mathrm{SSE} =& SST - SSR \\ S_\varepsilon^2 =& \frac{1}{N-2} SSE = \frac{1}{N - 2} (\mathrm{SST} - \mathrm{SSR}) \end{align} \]

Using Analysis of Variance

  • Summary:

    \[\begin{align} \mathrm{SST} =& \mathrm{SSR} + \mathrm{SSE} \\ \mathrm{SST} =& (N - 1) \times \text{total variance in } y \\ \mathrm{SSR} =& (N - 1) \beta_1^2 \times \text{total variance in } x \\ \mathrm{SSE} =& \text{sum of squared residuals} \end{align}\]

  • ANOVA Table

    Source df SS MS F
    Total \(N - 1\) \(\mathrm{SST}\)
    Regression \(1\) \(\mathrm{SSR}\) \[\mathrm{MSR} = \frac{\mathrm{SSR}}{1} \] \[ \frac{\mathrm{MSR}}{\mathrm{MSE}} \]
    Residuals \(N - 2\) \(\mathrm{SSE}\) \[\mathrm{MSE} = \frac{\mathrm{SSE}}{N - 2} = S_\varepsilon^2\]

  • Mean squared error

    \[ MSE = S_\varepsilon^2 = \frac{1}{N-2} \mathrm{SSE} \]

  • Coefficient of Determination \(R^2\)

    \[ R^2 = \frac{\mathrm{SSR}}{\mathrm{SST}} = 1 - \frac{\mathrm{SSE}}{\mathrm{SST}} \]

    \(R^2\) measures the fraction of the total variance explained by the regression model

  • \(F\) ratio:

    \[ F = \frac{\mathrm{MSR}}{\mathrm{MSE}} \]

Interpreting Unexplained Variance

Statistical and Systems Models

  • Systems model: tree-ring growth depends on temperature, soil moisture, …

    \[ \text{growth} = f(T, M, \ldots) \]

    • The dependence is approximately linear near some reference values

      \[ \begin{align} \text{growth}\: =&\: \alpha_0 + \alpha_T (T - T_0) + \\ &\: \alpha_M (M - M_0) + \ldots \end{align} \]

      • We simplify and look only at the linear terms in \(T\) and ignore the rest.

  • Statistical model

    \[ \begin{align} \text{growth}\: =&\: \alpha_0 + \alpha_T (T - T_0) + \ldots \\ T =&\: T_0 + \frac{1}{\alpha_T} (\text{growth} - \alpha_0 - \ldots) \\ =&\:\beta_0 + \beta_g\, \text{growth} + \varepsilon, \end{align} \]

    where \(\varepsilon\) represents all the terms in the real system that we’re ignoring.

    • Thus \(\varepsilon\) is the unexplained variance in \(T\).
      • All the variance that isn’t explained by the growth index.
